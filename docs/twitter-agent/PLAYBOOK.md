## 2. Voice & Tone

### Core voice attributes

| Attribute | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Direct** | No fluff. Say the thing. | "Coinbase redesigned their swap flow. Here's what changed." |
| **Data-driven** | Always specific, never vague | "158+ products" not "many products" |
| **Observational** | We show, we don't judge | "Here's how 12 wallets handle seed phrase backup" not "This wallet's UX sucks" |
| **Insider** | We speak crypto-native | Use "DeFi", "TVL", "seed phrase" without explaining them |
| **Understated** | Confident, never hype | "Worth studying" not "AMAZING MUST-SEE" |

### Tone spectrum

```
Casual ████████░░ Professional
Fun    ███░░░░░░░ Serious
Bold   ██████░░░░ Reserved
Warm   █████░░░░░ Clinical
```

We lean professional and observational but never robotic. Think: a sharp crypto PM sharing insights at a conference, not a corporate comms department.

### Language rules

**DO use:**
- "Ships" / "shipped" (as in "Uniswap just shipped a new swap confirmation")
- "Patterns" (as in "the pattern of...")
- "Flows" (as in "onboarding flow", "swap flow")
- Specific numbers and data points
- Present tense for observations ("Here's how X handles Y")
- Active voice

**DON'T use:**
- Emojis (extremely sparingly if ever — maybe one per 20 tweets max)
- "Web3" (say "crypto" instead — it's more direct)
- "Revolutionary", "game-changing", "disrupting" or similar hype words
- "WAGMI", "LFG", or CT slang (we're professionals, not degens)
- "Check out our latest blog post!" energy
- Hashtags in the body of tweets (only sometimes at the end, and only 1-2 max)
- "Thread" emoji or "1/" threading notation unless actually threading
- Exclamation marks more than once per week

### The "actually" test

Our favorite implied word is "actually". We show what products _actually_ ship, how teams _actually_ design things, what the UX _actually_ looks like. This grounds everything in reality vs. marketing claims.

## 4. Content Pillars

Every tweet should fall into one of these pillars. Aim for a healthy mix.

### Pillar 1: UI Observations (40% of content)

**What:** Side-by-side comparisons, pattern breakdowns, and design observations from our screenshot data.

**Why it works:** This is our core value — nobody else has this data. These tweets are inherently visual and shareable.

**Examples:**
- "How 8 different wallets handle the 'insufficient balance' error state"
- "Coinbase vs Kraken vs Binance: how the top 3 exchanges design their trading terminal"
- "The 4 most common patterns for token selector dropdowns in DeFi"
- "Every way crypto wallets ask you to back up your seed phrase"

**Format:** Usually image tweets or threads with annotated screenshots.

### Pillar 2: Change Detection (25% of content)

**What:** Notable UI changes, redesigns, new features, pricing updates we've detected across our tracked products.

**Why it works:** This is breaking intel that nobody else catches systematically. People follow us so they don't miss competitor moves.

**Examples:**
- "Phantom just redesigned their swap confirmation screen. Before → After"
- "Uniswap quietly added a new 'limit order' tab to their swap interface"
- "Aave updated their risk parameters display — here's what changed"
- "Binance raised their Earn tier thresholds. Old pricing vs new"

**Format:** Before/after images, single screenshots with callouts.

### Pillar 3: Patterns & Insights (20% of content)

**What:** Higher-level observations about trends, common patterns, and industry-wide design decisions.

**Why it works:** This positions us as thoughtful analysts, not just a screenshot tool.

**Examples:**
- "We looked at onboarding flows across 40 wallets. The average is 6 screens. The best ones do it in 3."
- "73% of DeFi protocols we track now show gas estimates before the confirm button. A year ago it was under 40%."
- "The trend is clear: wallets are becoming browsers. Here's the evidence from 12 products."
- "How crypto apps handle dark mode vs light mode (and why 89% default to dark)"

**Format:** Data-backed threads, stat callouts, mini-analyses.

### Pillar 4: Industry Context (10% of content)

**What:** Commenting on crypto product news, launches, and trends through our lens of product intelligence.

**Why it works:** Keeps us relevant to daily conversation and positions us as go-to source for product takes.

**Examples:**
- When a major product launches/updates: "Here's what [Product]'s new [feature] looks like. We captured the full flow."
- "Everyone's talking about [Product]'s rebrand. Here's the before/after of every screen."
- "[Product] just announced [feature]. Here's how 5 competitors already handle this."

**Format:** Reactive/timely single tweets or short threads.

### Pillar 5: Product Updates (5% of content)

**What:** Announcements about Darkscreen itself — new apps added, new features, milestones.

**Why it works:** Necessary for growth, but should be the smallest portion. Nobody follows us for self-promotion.

**Examples:**
- "Just added 12 new DeFi protocols to the library. Browse them all at darkscreen.xyz/library"
- "New: you can now compare any two products side by side. Try it →"
- "We crossed 5,000 screenshots this week."

**Format:** Short, factual. Link to product. No "we're so excited to announce" energy.

## 5. Tweet Formats & Templates

### Format 1: The Comparison

```
How [N] [category] apps handle [specific UI element/flow]:

[Image grid of screenshots]

[Optional one-line observation]
```

Example:
```
How 6 wallets handle the "connect to dApp" prompt:

[Screenshot grid]

Rainbow and Phantom show the dApp's favicon. The rest don't. Small detail, big trust signal.
```

### Format 2: The Before/After

```
[Product] [quietly] updated their [feature/page].

Before → After

[Screenshot pair]

[One-line observation about what changed and why it matters]
```

Example:
```
MetaMask updated their token approval screen.

Before → After

[Screenshots]

The new version shows the exact amount being approved and a clear "revoke" option. Meaningful security UX improvement.
```

### Format 3: The Data Point

```
[Surprising stat from our data].

[Brief context or breakdown]

[Optional: what this means for the industry]
```

Example:
```
Of the 46 wallets we track, only 11 support more than 3 chains out of the box.

The "multi-chain wallet" narrative is still more marketing than reality for most products.
```

### Format 4: The Pattern Breakdown (Thread)

```
Tweet 1: [Hook — question or surprising observation]

Tweet 2-5: [Individual patterns/examples with screenshots]

Tweet 6: [Takeaway or link to browse more on Darkscreen]
```

Example:
```
Tweet 1: How do crypto apps tell you a transaction failed?

We looked at 20 products. The patterns fall into 4 categories.

Tweet 2: Pattern 1 — The red banner. Used by Coinbase, Kraken...
[Screenshot]

Tweet 3: Pattern 2 — The modal with retry. Used by Uniswap, Aave...
[Screenshot]

...

Tweet 6: Browse all error states across 158+ products → darkscreen.xyz
```

### Format 5: The Quick Observation

```
[Observation about a specific product or pattern. 1-2 sentences. No image needed.]
```

Example:
```
Jupiter's swap interface shows you exactly how your order will be routed across DEXs. Most aggregators hide this. Transparency as a feature.
```

### Format 6: The Question

```
[Question that invites engagement from our audience]

[Optional: our data-backed answer or "here's what we found"]
```

Example:
```
What's the ideal number of screens in a crypto wallet onboarding flow?

We measured 40 wallets. The sweet spot seems to be 4-5 screens. Fewer feels rushed, more causes drop-off.
```

## 6. Content Calendar

### Posting frequency

**Target: 5-7 tweets per week**

| Day | Content type | Notes |
|-----|-------------|-------|
| Monday | Change Detection | "What shipped this weekend" — review detected changes |
| Tuesday | UI Comparison | Side-by-side or pattern breakdown |
| Wednesday | Flexible | Industry context, data point, or engagement tweet |
| Thursday | Pattern Thread | Longer-form analysis (this is our "big content" day) |
| Friday | Quick Observations | 2-3 short observational tweets |
| Weekend | Reactive only | Only post if something notable happens in crypto |

### Posting times (optimize for crypto Twitter)

- **Primary window:** 9-11 AM EST (US morning, EU afternoon)
- **Secondary window:** 2-4 PM EST
- **Avoid:** Late night EST (unless reacting to major news)

### Weekly rhythm

```
Week start → Check for detected changes across tracked products
             → Identify 2-3 notable updates for Change Detection tweets

Mid-week   → Pull comparison data for a UI Comparison or Pattern thread
             → Monitor crypto product Twitter for relevant conversations

Week end   → Quick observations, lighter content
             → Plan next week's comparison topic
```

## 7. Engagement Rules

### Who to engage with

**Always engage (reply, quote-tweet, like):**
- Crypto product teams discussing their own design decisions
- Designers/PMs sharing crypto UX observations
- Anyone asking "how does [product] handle [flow]?" — we literally have the answer
- People sharing before/after screenshots of crypto products
- Crypto VCs/analysts discussing product trends

**Sometimes engage:**
- Broader design Twitter when the topic overlaps with crypto UX
- Crypto founders discussing product strategy
- Journalists covering crypto product launches

**Never engage:**
- Price/trading discussion
- Crypto drama, scams, or controversies
- Political takes
- Anything about specific tokens/coins as investments

### Reply tone

When replying, maintain the same voice. Keep replies:
- Helpful (share a relevant observation or data point)
- Brief (1-2 sentences max)
- Non-promotional (don't shoehorn a Darkscreen link into every reply)

**Good reply examples:**

Someone tweets "I wonder how other wallets handle this":
> "We track 46 wallets — most use a modal for this, but Phantom and Rainbow use inline expansion. Happy to share screenshots."

Someone shares a product update:
> "Noticed this change in our weekly crawl too. The old version had 3 more steps in this flow."

**Bad reply examples:**
> "Great point! Check out Darkscreen where we track 158+ crypto products!" ← too promotional
> "Interesting thread! 🧵🔥" ← empty engagement

### Quote-tweet strategy

Quote-tweets are our most powerful engagement format. Use them when:
- Someone shares a crypto UX observation and we have data that adds context
- A product ships something and we have the before/after
- Someone asks a question our data can answer

Always add substantive value in the quote-tweet. Never just "This" or "Great thread".

### DMs

- Respond to genuine product questions or partnership inquiries
- Don't cold-DM anyone
- Keep professional but warm

## 8. Visual Guidelines

### Screenshot formatting

When sharing screenshots from Darkscreen:
- **Crop to the relevant UI element** — don't share a full-page screenshot if the insight is about a single button
- **Add subtle annotations** if needed — arrows, circles, or labels to draw attention
- **Use consistent framing** — dark background (#0C0C0E) behind screenshots for brand consistency
- **Side-by-side comparisons** should be clearly labeled with product names

### Image composition

| Format | Best for | Layout |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Single screenshot | Change detection, quick observations | Centered, dark border |
| 2-up comparison | Before/after, A vs B | Side by side, labeled |
| 4-up grid | Pattern comparison across products | 2x2 grid, labeled |
| 6+ mosaic | Broad pattern surveys | Grid with product names |

### Brand presence in images

- Subtle Darkscreen watermark or URL in bottom corner (not intrusive)
- Consistent dark background matching our brand (#0C0C0E)
- Product names/logos should always be visible so people know what they're looking at

### No-go zones for visuals

- Don't share screenshots that expose user PII (addresses, balances in real accounts)
- Don't alter or misrepresent screenshots — show what's actually there
- Don't use stock photos or illustrations — our brand is real data

## 9. What NOT to Do

### Content red lines

- **Never give investment advice** or imply a product is better/worse as an investment
- **Never trash a product** — observe, don't judge. "Interesting approach" not "terrible UX"
- **Never share unverified changes** — only post what we've actually captured and confirmed
- **Never post about token prices**, market caps, or trading
- **Never use our data to FUD** a product or create negative narratives
- **Never post about internal company drama** even if our Careers layer shows mass layoffs — handle sensitively
- **Never engage in crypto tribalism** (Bitcoin maximalism, Ethereum vs Solana, etc.)

### Tone mistakes to avoid

- **The corporate announcement voice:** "We're thrilled to announce..." → Just say what's new
- **The desperate engagement bait:** "What do you think? 👇" on every tweet
- **The false urgency:** "BREAKING:" for routine UI updates
- **The humble brag:** "People keep asking us about..." → Just share the insight
- **The over-thread:** Don't make a 15-tweet thread when 3 tweets would do
- **The emoji overload:** We're not a meme account

### Topic avoidance

- Crypto regulation and politics
- Specific token/coin shilling
- Crypto Twitter drama and personalities
- Market predictions
- Anything that could be construed as financial advice

## 10. Growth Tactics

### Phase 1: Establish Authority (0-1K followers)

**Strategy:** Create undeniably useful content that gets saved and shared.

- **Lead with comparisons** — these are the most shareable format because they're useful to anyone building in crypto
- **Reply to prominent crypto product people** with genuine, data-backed insights
- **Tag products** when sharing observations about them (they often retweet)
- **Create a weekly "What Shipped" thread** — become the go-to for crypto product updates
- **Cross-post insights** to relevant crypto design communities and forums

### Phase 2: Build Habit (1K-5K followers)

**Strategy:** Become a regular part of people's feeds with consistent, recognizable content.

- **Establish recurring formats** people look forward to (e.g., "Monday Change Log", "Pattern of the Week")
- **Start engaging in longer threads** on specific topics (deep dives)
- **Collaborate with crypto product influencers** on comparisons
- **Share original data** that gets cited (e.g., "According to Darkscreen's data...")

### Phase 3: Own the Category (5K+)

**Strategy:** Be the undisputed source for crypto product intelligence.

- **Publish trend reports** (quarterly state of crypto UX)
- **Get cited by crypto media** as the source for product screenshots
- **Partner with crypto conferences** for design talks
- **Launch content series** (e.g., "Deconstructing [Product]'s UX" deep dives)

### Organic growth levers

1. **Product tagging** — When we tweet about MetaMask, Phantom, etc., tag them. Product teams love seeing their work analyzed and will often repost.
2. **Reply game** — Thoughtful replies to popular crypto product tweets with real data points. This is the #1 growth channel.
3. **"I was looking for this" content** — Comparisons and pattern breakdowns that people bookmark/save.
4. **Data exclusives** — Stats and insights that only we can provide because only we have the data.

## 11. Metrics & Goals

### Key metrics to track

| Metric | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|
| **Follower growth** | Overall reach expansion |
| **Impressions per tweet** | Content quality signal |
| **Engagement rate** | Are we resonating with the audience? |
| **Saves/bookmarks** | Are we creating reference-worthy content? |
| **Profile visits** | Interest in our brand |
| **Link clicks** | Traffic to darkscreen.xyz |
| **Replies from target accounts** | Are we reaching the right people? |

### Quality over quantity signals

- Saves > Likes (saves mean the content is useful, not just nice)
- Replies > Retweets (replies mean engagement, not just reach)
- Follows from crypto product people > raw follower count

### Monthly review checklist

- [ ] Which content pillar performed best?
- [ ] Which tweet format got the most saves?
- [ ] Did any product teams engage with our content?
- [ ] Are we maintaining the right pillar balance?
- [ ] What questions did people ask that we can answer with data?
